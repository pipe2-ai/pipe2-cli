## Why it works

A repeated silhouette gives the eye somewhere to land when the environment changes. The contrast comes from the sets, not from turning your product into something else. Choose three worlds with different colors, textures and scale, then keep the camera and product placement consistent.

GPT Image 2 establishes an original design and its first composition. Nano Banana 2 edits that reference for each new set. Both edits start from the original image: the third scene does not inherit accidental changes from the second. Seedance 2.5 receives all three images as ordered references for a nine-second, 720p vertical campaign. These references guide the shots; they are not exact-frame guarantees.

The default bottle moves from peach dunes to an emerald forest and finally a moonlit cloudscape. This is an imaginary concept campaign, not evidence that a real product was photographed in those places.

## The knobs that matter

- **`--product`** — use a distinctive but simple shape. Fine print, complex closures and transparent internals make consistency harder to judge.
- **`--scene-one`** — make the opening understandable without an introduction. Keep the product visible immediately.
- **`--scene-three`** — give the final set the strongest sense of space or contrast. A final hold should reward watching the sequence.

## Notes

Check the cap, edges and proportions across every cut. Reference-guided generation can still redesign small details, so do not present an approximate concept as an exact commercial packshot. Keep all three scene descriptions compatible with the same product placement.

The result is intentionally silent. Add properly licensed audio when preparing your post, and disclose that the campaign is AI-generated. This recipe does not promise a seamless loop or frame-identical results on reruns.
